Single-crystal diffraction

Monochromatic beam (wavelength λ). A reflection hkl appears when its reciprocal-lattice point lies on the Ewald sphere of radius 1/λ. Rotate or oscillate the crystal with the goniometer to bring points onto the sphere, and record the spots on the detector.

Lattice parameters
Range (h, k, l): min, max
Only lattice-centring extinctions are applied (no glide planes or screw axes).

Mosaic spread Δω turns each reciprocal point into a small cap of radius |g|·Δω/2: the reflection diffracts while the cap touches the Ewald sphere, and spots grow at high angle. B is a Debye–Waller factor: intensities fall as exp(−B|g|²/2).

Display
Detector
The angle 2θD swings the detector about the vertical axis through the crystal (0° = transmission, 180° = back-reflection). Click a spot in the inset to select it; drag the inset's lower-left corner to resize it.
Integration
While Integrate is on, every reflection that crosses the Ewald sphere is recorded (yellow); cyan rings are spots diffracting now. Turning Integrate on starts the ω scan if it is paused, and pauses it again when integration stops. Recorded spots stay on the detector when you move it; a new wavelength or a new lattice clears them.
